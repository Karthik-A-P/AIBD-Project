  What it does
  - Gene columns: It takes every column after ICU as a gene, which is about
    17,700 columns.
  - Groups: It splits samples by the first letter of the ID in Unnamed: 0: C
    (100 samples) and N (85 samples).
  - Test: It runs a two-sided Welch's t-test on each gene.
  - Correction: It applies the Benjamini–Hochberg correction at α = 0.05, which
    is the 95% level. A gene counts as significant if its adjusted p-value is
    below 0.05.
  - Direction and size: For each gene, the percent change in mean expression is
    (mean C − mean N) / mean N × 100. Significant genes are labelled elevated or
    suppressed by whether the C mean is above or below the N mean.
  - Output: It prints the number and percentage of significant genes, split into
    elevated and suppressed. It also prints the median percent change for each
    direction and shows the top 10 genes by adjusted p-value.

  Assumptions to check
  - Group direction: I treated C as the case group and N as the control, so
    "elevated" means higher in C than in N. Swap the two if you meant the
    reverse.
  - Percent change scale: The values look like raw or normalised expression, not
    log values, so I computed percent change directly on the means. If the data
    are log-scaled, tell me and I'll change it.
  - Skipped genes: A gene with no variance in either group gets no p-value, so
    it is left out of the correction and the "genes tested" count.

  You'll also need statsmodels installed. If it isn't, run pip install
  statsmodels.
